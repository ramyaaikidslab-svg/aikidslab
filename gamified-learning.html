AI Reflection Quest

Students learn to tell real AI from plain automation, then go hands-on with each domain. They play against an AI that learns their habits and try to make a biased AI fair, paint pixels to see an image the way a computer does and make a vision AI unsure, and fool a mood-reading AI with sarcasm and Hinglish. Every wrong answer comes back as a new question until it's fixed, and a level only unlocks the next at 80%. At the end they beat a boss robot and design an AI of their own, with a certificate to download.

  • 6 levels + finale

What it teaches

Three domains of AI, each learned by doing.

Each domain gets its own lab, with a toy to play with before the idea gets its name. The memory hook students leave with: Data counts, Vision sees, Language talks.

Domain one

Data

A Rock-Paper-Scissors AI starts knowing nothing, studies the student’s moves, and starts winning. Then: weather forecasts, fraud alerts, delivery times and IPL win predictors.

Domain two

Computer Vision

Students paint on a pixel grid and watch it turn into the numbers a computer stores, then guess pictures from a handful of pixels. Then: face unlock, crop-disease apps and India’s tiger count.

Domain three

Natural Language Processing

A mood-reading AI is made happy, then sad, then flipped with “not”, then fooled with sarcasm. Students build a sentence one predicted word at a time, the way a chatbot does.

Level 1 · ~8 min

AI or Not? A Day Full of AI

Ask a calculator and a maps app the same question three times. Only one answer changes — that’s the test for AI. Then find it hiding in a school day.

Level 2 · ~9 min

Data Lab

Play an AI that learns your habits, then fix a kabaddi-team picker that learned from one-sided data.

Level 3 · ~10 min

Vision Lab

Pixels become numbers, edges become shapes — then dim the light and turn the photo to make the AI unsure.

Level 4 · ~10 min

Language Lab

Mood missions with sarcasm and Hinglish, a next-word keyboard, and the steps inside “Hey Google, aaj barish hogi?”

Level 5 · ~9 min

Sort & Mash-up

Speed-sort real apps, take apart multi-domain systems, and be the human who checks an AI’s decisions.

Level 6 · ~8 min

Boss: GLITCH

A mistake review with “why?” questions, then a boss battle that draws on everything learned.

Finale · ~6 min

Design your own AI

Pick a real problem, choose the data, think about who could be treated unfairly, and download your idea card and certificate.

Built in

Mistakes that come back

Every wrong answer is tagged with the misunderstanding behind it and returns as a different question until it’s fixed — even on the next visit.

Ways to use it

For a child at home, or a whole class.

At home

A child can play alone from start to finish. There’s a natural pause after Level 3, and progress saves in their browser, so they can come back later on the same device and start by fixing the ideas they missed.

In a classroom

Put it on the projector and let the class vote on each answer, or let pairs play on lab computers. The boss battle works well as a whole-class finish.

After playing

The finale ends with three reflection questions, including what could go wrong if an AI learns from unfair data. They make a good ten-minute discussion.
