The curriculum

Three grades. Three different programs. Three different capstones.

A Grade 7 student and a Grade 9 student should not be handed the same worksheet. Each year builds deliberately on the last — from understanding how AI thinks, to shipping a working product, to standing up and pitching it like a founder.

Grade 7 · Year One

AI Literate

Understand it, direct it, train your first model.

What they learn

  • How AI actually works — data, patterns and prediction, taught without the maths jargon
  • Prompting as a real skill: directing ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini to do useful work
  • Training their first model themselves, on images, sound or gestures
  • Creative AI: images, audio and storytelling, with a critical eye
  • Safety and ethics: bias, deepfakes, and what never to share with a model
  • First builds with block-based and no-code tools
Teachable MachineScratch + AIChatGPTClaudeGeminiCanva AI
Capstone AI for my classroom

A trained model or simple assistant that solves a problem inside their own school — a waste-segregation sorter, a lost-and-found identifier, a revision helper for their toughest subject. Built, tested, and presented to their class.

Grade 8 · Year Two

AI Builder

Turn a model into a product people can use.

What they learn

  • From model to product: how an AI application is actually assembled
  • Working with data — collecting, cleaning and labelling a dataset of their own
  • Building real apps with vibe-coding and no-code tools
  • Python foundations: enough to read, debug and change what the AI writes
  • Assistants that answer from their own documents and notes
  • Testing with real users: does this work for anyone other than me?
ReplitLovableCursorPython basicsMIT App InventorHugging Face
Capstone AI for my community

A working AI app or assistant aimed at a real problem beyond the classroom — a local-language study helper, a plant-disease checker for a family farm, a safety or accessibility tool. Shipped, put in front of real users, and improved on their feedback.

Grade 9 · Year Three

Young Founder

Find a real problem. Build the MVP. Pitch it.

What they learn

  • Finding a problem worth solving, and talking to the people who have it
  • Product thinking: scope, MVP, and deciding what to build first
  • Building an end-to-end product with AI tooling, start to finish
  • Measuring what matters — is anyone actually using this?
  • Business basics: who pays, what it costs, how it could grow
  • The pitch: telling the whole story in five minutes, on a stage

A working MVP with real users, a set of numbers behind it, and a founder's pitch delivered to a panel of industry mentors. The strongest of these go forward to the Build-a-thon — and, as our incubator partnerships come online, to student incubators.

Two schools, five years from now

The same AI line in the syllabus, delivered two very different ways.

AI as a textbook chapter

  • Students can define what AI is, and have never built anything with it
  • The subject rests entirely on one teacher who was never trained for it
  • The same content is taught to Grade 7 and Grade 9 with the slides barely changed
  • At an admissions meeting, "we cover AI" sounds like every other school in the city
  • Nothing to show parents at the end of the year but marks

AI as something they build

  • Every student in Grades 7–9 finishes the year with a working AI project of their own
  • Your faculty are trained and supported, with an industry expert on campus monthly
  • Grade 7, 8 and 9 each run a distinct curriculum and a distinct capstone
  • You can name the products your students shipped, and show the demo video
  • An annual Build-a-thon, an award ceremony, and students on a path towards incubators

We're not teaching children to use AI. We're raising the founders who will build with it.

India does not have a shortage of bright children. It has a shortage of places where a fourteen-year-old is taken seriously enough to build something real and be asked who it is for. Our mission is to bring AI education into schools in a form that produces builders — students who leave Grade 9 having identified a genuine problem, shipped something that works, and stood in front of a room to defend it. Not because it fills a line in the syllabus, but because the next decade belongs to people who can build, and India should be producing them from the classroom up.

On our roadmap

Student incubator partnerships across India. We're building collaborations with student incubators so that the strongest school projects don't stop at the award ceremony. A Grade 9 student with a working product and real users should have a route to turning it into a business — and the school that started them gets to say so.

Questions school leaders ask

Which grades does this cover? +
Grades 7, 8 and 9, with a distinct curriculum and a distinct capstone project for each year. Grade 7 builds AI literacy and trains a first model, Grade 8 ships a working application, and Grade 9 works like a founder — problem, MVP, users and a pitch.
How much timetable time does it need? +
One 45–60 minute period a week, usually the computer or skill period you already have, plus one full expert day a month. There is no parallel timetable, no weekend classes and no additional teaching staff to hire.
Will this clash with our board syllabus? +
No — it's designed to embed into it. Before launch we run a curriculum mapping session with your academic coordinator and align every module against your existing AI, computer science and computational thinking units, whether you run CBSE, ICSE, IB, Cambridge or a state board.
Does it add to our teachers' workload? +
The teaching content is self-paced and recorded, so your teacher facilitates the period rather than preparing it. We also run a teacher enablement track so your faculty genuinely grow into the subject instead of being sidelined by an outside vendor.
What infrastructure do we need? +
An internet-connected computer lab with modern browsers, and ideally one device per one or two students. Everything runs in the cloud. No GPUs, no servers, no new hardware purchase.
What actually happens on the monthly expert day? +
An industry engineer spends the day on your campus. The morning is a doubt-clearing clinic on that month's modules, the middle of the day is a live build sprint on the class project with the expert on the floor, and before they leave, every team demos what moved that day. It's a working session, not a guest lecture.
How are students assessed? +
Each capstone carries a written rubric covering understanding, build quality, problem fit and presentation, alongside module completion tracked per student. Schools typically feed this straight into internal assessment and the term report card.
What does it cost? +
Pricing is per school, based on the grades you cover and your student numbers, and covers the curriculum, the platform, teacher enablement, the monthly expert days and the annual Build-a-thon. We share the full commercials on the demo call.
Can we pilot with one grade first? +
Yes, and many schools do. Start with a single grade for one term, see the capstones the students produce, and extend to all three grades from the next academic year.
Are the experts who come on campus verified? +
Every expert is background-verified and assessed on how well they work with 12–15 year olds, not just on their engineering résumé. Our full child-safety policy, consent process and campus protocol are shared before you sign anything.
What about student data and online safety? +
Tools and accounts are chosen for this age group, students don't share personal data with models, and safe, ethical AI use is taught as part of the curriculum from Grade 7 onwards. We share our data handling and tool list with your team during onboarding.
Do students need prior coding experience? +
No. Grade 7 starts from zero and is built for students who have never written a line of code. Python appears only in Grade 8, and only as much as a student needs to read and change what AI writes for them.
What do parents see? +
A term-wise progress report for their child, the capstone their child built and presented, and an invitation to the annual Build-a-thon and award ceremony. We'll also run a parent orientation session at launch if you'd like one.
How long does it take to launch? +
Roughly four weeks from agreement: curriculum mapping, teacher enablement, lab and account setup, then classes begin, with the first expert day landing inside the first month.
Do you work with schools outside Hyderabad? +
The curriculum and platform run anywhere in India. The monthly on-campus expert day is currently strongest in and around Hyderabad, and we're onboarding schools in other cities as we build the expert network there — tell us your city on the call and we'll be straight with you about what we can commit to.
What happens to the strongest student projects? +
They're selected at your school's annual Build-a-thon, judged by industry mentors and recognised at an award ceremony. On our roadmap, we're building partnerships with student incubators across India so that a Grade 9 student with a working product and real users has a route to turning it into an actual business.
