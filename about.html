About AI Kids Lab

We build AI education inside schools, not around them.

Weekend programs reach the children whose parents already knew to look. A school reaches all of them. That difference is the whole reason this company works the way it does.

Why we started

It began as a weekend cohort for fifteen children.

AI Kids Lab started because our founder, Ramya, was not willing to let her own two children learn AI from a rushed module or a generic app. The first version was exactly what you would expect: a small, in-person cohort in Hyderabad, fifteen seats, admission by interview, mentors who had actually shipped AI in production.

It worked. Children who had never written a line of code were training their own models within weeks and presenting working projects to a room of parents at the end.

But something about it kept bothering us. Every child in that room already had a parent who had gone looking. A parent who had read the headlines, searched, compared programs, and paid. The children who most needed to understand what AI is — and who it is being built by — were not the ones sitting in front of us.

A weekend program is a filter. A school is not. Inside a school, the curious child and the quiet one and the one whose parents have never heard the word "capstone" all get the same year.

So we rebuilt the whole thing for schools. The same mentors, the same insistence on building rather than watching, but delivered as a proper grade-wise curriculum that sits inside your existing timetable and reaches every student in Grades 7, 8 and 9 — not the fifteen whose parents found us.

That is the company now. We are not an enrichment class that schools can refer parents to. We are the AI curriculum your school runs, with the people who build this technology walking into your classrooms every month.

What we believe

Six things we will not compromise on.

These are not values on a wall. Each one changes what a Tuesday afternoon in your computer lab actually looks like.

01

The engine before the app

Most AI-for-kids courses start with an app template and an API key. It feels productive and teaches almost nothing, because an app is mostly screens and logins. Our students build the model first, then solve a problem with it, and add the interface last — which turns out to be the easy part.

02

Every student, not the keen few

We license per grade, so the whole cohort is included. AI literacy should not be an opt-in for the children whose parents pushed. The quiet student in the third row gets the same year as the one who already builds robots at home.

03

Taught by people who ship

Nothing enters a module until it has genuinely been built somewhere first. The experts who come to your campus are working engineers from real product teams, not trainers reading from a deck they were handed last week.

04

Honest evaluation over demos

A demo that works once, on the presenter's laptop, with the one input they tested, teaches a child the wrong lesson. Every project carries a model card that states plainly what it gets wrong and where it fails. Saying "this is only 71% accurate on faces it hasn't seen" is the skill.

05

Safeguarding is not a footnote

No student ever needs a personal AI account. Every model call runs through our managed gateway. Sensitive projects use scripted, facilitator-reviewed examples only, and nothing about a student is published without written parental consent.

06

The school owns the outcome

We train your faculty to run the weekly period rather than replacing them with an outside vendor who leaves when the contract ends. The capstones, the rubrics, the Build-a-thon photographs and the admissions story all belong to your school.

Where we work

Hyderabad first, then across India.

The curriculum and the platform run anywhere with an internet-connected lab. The monthly on-campus expert day is strongest in and around Hyderabad, and we are building the expert network city by city rather than promising coverage we cannot staff.

  • Grades 7, 8 and 9. A distinct curriculum and a distinct capstone for each year. A Grade 10 lighter-load variant is available where a school wants it, timed against board preparation.
  • Any board. CBSE, ICSE, IB, Cambridge or state. We map our modules onto your existing syllabus before the first class rather than running a second one beside it.
  • Licensed per grade cohort. Every student in that year is included, and each new intake is covered automatically without a fresh conversation.
  • A named contact, always. Every partner school gets a named grievance and safeguarding contact, and a coordinator dashboard that shows progress student by student.
