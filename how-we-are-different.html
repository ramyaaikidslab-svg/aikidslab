How we're different

You have five realistic options. Four of them are cheaper than us.

So it is worth being precise about what the difference actually buys, and equally precise about when one of the other four is genuinely the right call for your school.

The options

What schools actually choose between.

Not brands — models. Almost every AI offering a school is pitched falls into one of these, and the model matters far more than the logo on it.

Option one

The textbook chapter

AI as a unit in the computer science book, taught in a handful of periods, assessed by a written test.

  • No additional cost
  • Fits the timetable perfectly
  • Students can define AI and have built nothing
Option two

Your own CS faculty

Ask the computer science department to build and teach an AI program themselves.

  • Your staff own it completely
  • Costs a training budget, not a licence
  • Asks a teacher to write a curriculum in a field that changes every few months, on top of a full load
Option three

A robotics or STEM lab vendor

Kits, equipment and an activity curriculum, often tied to an Atal Tinkering Lab.

  • Genuinely good for hands-on engineering
  • Visible, and parents like seeing the lab
  • Mostly hardware and electronics; the AI content is usually thin
Option four

One-off workshops

An external trainer for a day or a week, once or twice a year, often ending in certificates.

  • Cheap, easy to arrange, photographs well
  • Low risk and low commitment
  • Nothing compounds between visits, so nothing is built
Option five

A generic online course licence

Seats on a self-paced video platform, assigned to students to work through.

  • Scales to any number of students
  • Low cost per head
  • Completion rates without a live human in the loop are the well-known weakness
What we do

A grade-wise in-school curriculum

Three distinct years, self-paced modules in one existing period, a working engineer on campus monthly, and a capstone per grade.

  • Every student in Grades 7–9, not a club
  • Your teacher facilitates and is trained, not replaced
  • The most expensive option on this page

Being straight about it

When you should not pick us.

We would rather lose a school honestly than onboard one that will be unhappy by December.

  • If you only need to satisfy the syllabus, a textbook unit and a well-chosen online licence will do that at a fraction of the cost. Do not pay for a three-year program to tick a box.
  • If your computer lab cannot get reliable internet, nothing here works. Fix the connectivity first; we will still be here.
  • If you cannot protect one period a week, the program will quietly die by December and you will have paid for it. This needs a slot on the timetable, not goodwill.
  • If nobody on staff will own it, it will not survive. A named coordinator is not optional, and it is the one thing we cannot supply for you.
  • Pick us if you want every student in three grades to finish each year with something they built and can defend — and you want the strongest of them to have somewhere real to take it.
The four real differences

If you remember nothing else from this page.

01

Three grades, three curricula

Grade 7, 8 and 9 each run different content and a different capstone. Most alternatives run one program with three labels.

02

A practitioner on campus monthly

Not a guest lecture. A doubt clinic, a live build sprint, and a showcase before they leave that day.

03

No student AI accounts, ever

Every model call runs through our managed gateway. No twelve-year-old signs up for anything.

04

A route out of the building

Build-a-thon, then national competitions, then the student incubation labs we collaborate with.
