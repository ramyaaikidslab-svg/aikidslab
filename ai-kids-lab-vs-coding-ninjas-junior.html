Comparison for schools

AI Kids Lab vs Coding Ninjas Junior

Written for a founder, principal or academic coordinator — not for a parent. The short version: these are products for different buyers, and the comparison only makes sense once that is clear.

The honest framing

One is bought by a parent. One is licensed by a school.

Coding Ninjas Junior is, in essence, structured coding courses for children. AI Kids Lab is a grade-wise curriculum a school licenses for every student in Grades 7, 8 and 9. Neither is a better version of the other; they answer different questions.

Coding Ninjas Junior

Sold to the family

  • Focused on programming foundations and problem solving
  • Structured, sequenced, and genuinely good at what it targets
  • Typically purchased per learner
  • Programming is the subject; AI is a topic within it
AI Kids Lab

Licensed by the school

  • Licensed per grade cohort — every student in Grades 7–9 included
  • Runs inside one existing period a week, on your timetable
  • An industry engineer on your campus every month
  • A different curriculum and a different capstone for each of the three years
The practical difference

Coverage and accountability

  • A consumer product reaches the families who bought it
  • A school licence reaches the whole grade, including the students nobody signed up
  • Your faculty are trained rather than bypassed
  • The rubrics, projects and event material belong to your school

Being straight about it

When Coding Ninjas Junior is the better answer

Your priority is competitive programming or computer science fundamentals. Those are valuable and we do not replace them — our students write only as much Python as their models need.

  • They are not competitors for the same slot. A parent-bought class sits beside your school day. We sit inside it. A school can perfectly well have both.
  • The question to ask yourself is not "which is better" but "am I solving for the keen few, or for the whole grade?" That answer picks the model for you.
  • If it is the whole grade, see how we compare against the other in-school options — including the cheaper ones we sometimes tell schools to take.
